Pond started with one idea: the things you save online should end up somewhere you own. Not in a feed that reorders itself, and not behind an account that can close. In a folder on your computer.

Pond 2.0 keeps that idea and widens it. It reaches more of the places you save from, it reads the archives those places let you download, and your fish can now think on-device.

## Eighteen sources

Pond 1 synced nine sources. Pond 2.0 adds nine more:

- **GitHub** syncs the repositories you star and keeps each README to read in Pond.
- **Substack** syncs your saved and liked posts, and checks the publications you follow for new posts every six hours.
- **Tumblr** syncs your likes with the time you liked each post.
- **Savee** syncs the items you saved, newest first.
- **Behance** syncs your appreciated projects with the date you appreciated them.
- **Vimeo** syncs your likes and your Watch Later list.
- **Dribbble** syncs the shots you liked.
- **Unsplash** syncs your bookmarked photos.
- **Bluesky** syncs your public likes from your handle, and your bookmarks once you sign in.

Every source runs through your own browser session, on your own machine. There's no Pond server in the middle, and Pond paces each request so your account looks like you browsing. [See every source](/sources).

## Bring your history with you

Most platforms let you download an archive of your data. Until now, that archive sat in your Downloads folder.

**Import Data Export** reads the archives from X, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and Reddit, and adds every like and save to your library with its original date when the archive has one. YouTube playlists from Google Takeout become folders.

Pond still imports Eagle libraries, mymind, browser bookmarks, ArtStation, Huaban, Pixave, and Inboard exports, and plain folders of files. [See every format](/formats).

## Fish on your Mac

Fish are the helpers in Pond's right column. They search your library, sort saves into folders, and answer questions about what you kept.

On macOS 26, a fish can run on **Apple Intelligence**, the model built into your Mac. There's no key to paste and no host to run. Your library never leaves the computer.

You can still give a fish your own key, or point it at a local host like Ollama or LM Studio. In 2.0, fish can also call tools from **MCP servers you run**. Anything that could change your library waits for your review first. [Meet the fish](/fish).

## Save from anywhere

- The **Share menu** in any Mac app now has Pond, for links, images, and files.
- **Shortcuts** can save a link with `pond://save`, with tags and a folder, in the background.
- The **Alfred workflow** searches your library with `pond` and saves a link with `psave`.

The browser extension, the Raycast extension, the CLI, and the local API all work the same as before. [See every way to save](/integrations).

## Get it

Pond 2.0 is free and open source. It runs on macOS 13 or later and 64-bit Windows 10 or 11, and it updates itself.

[Download Pond](/download), or read the full [release notes](/changelog/2.0.0).
